What haemoglobin actually measures
Reviewed by Dr S. Iyer · TS/12345/2019
Last checked 02 Aug 2026

Haemoglobin is the protein in your blood that carries oxygen from your lungs to everywhere else. A lab measures how much of it is in a fixed volume of blood.

When it is low, people often feel tired in a way that sleep does not fix, get out of breath on stairs they used to manage, or look paler than usual. Many people feel nothing at all.

Your own reports use the range printed by the lab that ran them. Two labs can print different ranges for the same test, which is why we never substitute a standard one.

Low haemoglobin has many causes. Some are simple to treat and some are not, and the difference is not something you can work out from the number alone.

Talk to a doctor about this
This article explains a test. It cannot tell you what your result means.

Dev notes and edge cases

The decisions in this flow that are judgement calls rather than layout.
FINITE
A shelf, not a feed
No infinite scroll and no recommendation engine chasing attention. The library is small enough to finish, which is the honest shape for a product a student should open four times a year.
REVIEWED
Named clinician, dated
Every article carries a reviewer, their registration number and the date it was last checked. An unreviewed article does not publish, and one that goes stale is pulled rather than left up.
ENDS IN A HUMAN
No article about a symptom is a dead end
Each one closes with a booking route and a line saying what the article cannot do. Explaining a test without offering a way to act on it is the failure mode this rule prevents.
LINKED, NOT INFERRED
"Because of your last report" is a link, not a diagnosis
The suggestion is keyed to a test name that appears in the vault. It never interprets the value, never predicts, and can be turned off in settings.
INDIC FIRST
Designed in Telugu, then English
Telugu sets 15–25% longer and needs +2 line height. Designing in English first guarantees a cramped translation, so the longest language sets the layout.
EDGE CASES
The ones that will happen
Reviewer leaves the panel · guidance changes after publication · student reads an article about a condition they do not have · article suggested from a record later deleted · Telugu string overflows a button · figures rendered in Devanagari numerals by an over-eager translator.
